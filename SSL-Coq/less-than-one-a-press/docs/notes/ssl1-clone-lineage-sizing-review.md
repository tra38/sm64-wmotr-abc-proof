# SSL Area 1: which clone lineages actually have a replacement source?

This is a source review, not a controller replay or a new universal Coq theorem. It uses stock revision `9921382a68bb0c865e5e45eb594d9c64db59b1af`, the actual generated US/JP inventory already checked by `InkBounceProducerEffect.v`, and the corresponding C callbacks. The reviewed actor, allocation, carry-script and object-update files match that pin. Local `SSL_SPAWNING_DISPLACEMENT_TAS_HACK` additions in `area.c` and `ssl/script.c` are excluded. The chosen-position grant lets us place a successfully obtained clone at chosen finite, in-bounds X/Y/Z for a transfer test. It does not grant its species, size, initialization, tangibility, or the slot history needed to obtain it.

## A fake pickup needs a new occupant, not just a missing whitelist

`act_picking_up` explicitly documents the trick: the remembered object can unload during its animation, allowing Mario to grab the vacant or newly loaded slot. `mario_grab_used_object` has no species whitelist. Nevertheless, this does not establish that each enemy can become that new occupant. A stock spawn operation has to allocate the remembered slot before the grab completes, with the required constructor and contact state.

For a non-holdable occupant, `obj_set_held_state` redirects its current behavior command to a carry script. Each relevant carry script reaches `BREAK`; it does not rerun the species constructor, rescale the object or refresh its hitbox. Subsequent object-flag processing still runs, so “frozen” here means the native behavior and size updates stop, not that every field in the object stops changing. Drop/throw change position and the carry script, but do not resize the hitbox.

## Walking away does not reload the entry enemies

The macro loader creates entry actors when area terrain loads. The scripted spawn-info loader runs from `load_area` or the separate Mario spawn operation. Neither loader is a distance-triggered respawner. For ordinary non-roomed actors, the common behavior update hides distant objects and sets `ACTIVE_FLAG_FAR_AWAY`; that operation does not unload their pool slots. In particular, it does not arrange a newly allocated Goomba, Fly Guy or Klepto on returning.

A normal area unload/reload does free and recreate entry actors, subject to their respawn records. However, normal warp initialization calls `init_mario`, clearing `heldObj` and `usedObj`, resetting the action, and subsequently assigning `usedObj` to the spawn warp. It does not retain a pending old `ACT_PICKING_UP` across that reload. SSL Area 1 has no instant-warp table of the kind that can change areas while preserving the action; its instant-warp declarations belong to Areas 2 and 3. These observations obstruct that particular reload-and-grab proposal. They are not a proof excluding every possible cloning history.

## Actual replacement opportunities

“Candidate” in this table means that the source contains an in-area allocation of the species. It does not mean a no-A pickup, exact free-list slot, or usable clone has been reached.

For a concrete fake-slot experiment, Pokey parts are the first bounce-enemy candidate because their parent really does allocate new parts in SSL1. Flame children and explosions come next as hazard recipients, with different interaction effects. Bob-ombs and the small box are the strongest donor/replacement candidates because their grabbable and respawner paths are explicit. Goomba, Fly Guy and Klepto are lower-priority conditional transfers: their entry presence and the absent grab whitelist do not supply the needed in-area allocation. This ordering ranks the available source connections, not the probability of a successful gameplay route.

The allocator consumes the first free-list slot; freeing places a slot at the front. Other deletions and particle/child spawns can change which slot is consumed. Mario updates before the pushable and general-actor lists; most named recipient allocations occur after his callback, and deactivated objects unload at the end of the update. A recipient spawned after Mario's grab cannot satisfy that same grab. This ordering is useful for constructing or rejecting a concrete schedule, but a list of allocation sites does not itself construct one. See [allocator/free list](https://github.com/n64decomp/sm64/blob/9921382a68bb0c865e5e45eb594d9c64db59b1af/src/game/spawn_object.c#L79), [update order](https://github.com/n64decomp/sm64/blob/9921382a68bb0c865e5e45eb594d9c64db59b1af/src/game/object_list_processor.c#L172), and [complete frame ordering](https://github.com/n64decomp/sm64/blob/9921382a68bb0c865e5e45eb594d9c64db59b1af/src/game/object_list_processor.c#L647).

## Size lineage: a replacement does not inherit the donor's giant hitbox

Allocation clears the raw data and installs height 100, radius 50, down offset 0, interaction type 0 and intangible timer -1. Common graphical initialization resets scale to `(1,1,1)`. Ordinary `spawn_object` copies position and angle, not scale or live hitbox dimensions. Explicit scale-spawn helpers add their named scale afterward. Thus a newly allocated enemy does not retain the old donor's enlarged scale or hitbox just because its address is reused. A pre-constructor recipient has the generic height 100, but its negative intangible timer is not an eligible stock enemy contact; switching it to `BREAK` before initialization does not supply the missing interaction type or tangibility.

These are source-derived bounds for the named lineages, not Coq-discharge of every live history. The remaining connection is specific: prove that the actual recipient received the normal initialization, its constructor parameter/parent sizing record remained valid, the reached graphics/hitbox writes have those receivers, and no intervening ordinary writer changed the matched contact fields. Carry-script suppression preserves an existing size; it cannot stand in for a missing earlier initialization or lineage proof. The Fire Fly Guy needs separate signed-dimension/contact analysis rather than a blanket height-250 or absolute-size assumption.

## What this changes for the Ink comparison

Chosen placement cannot make Mario at movement Y 768 pass the hit-from-above guard against an enemy origin above 768. With synchronized incoming movement/collision/display records, the best fresh upward snap places the origin immediately below movement Y; its new signed rise approaches that enemy's live height, subject to Float32 rounding. The largest named positive bounce height in this inventory is Klepto's 250, while the actual in-area Pokey replacement candidate is at most 60 along its reviewed lineage. Neither reaches a fresh 1,093-unit high-movement/low-collision variant gap or the 1,170.864868-unit raised-display example by position selection alone. Those conclusions are conditional on the live size/contact lineage, not on an arbitrary granted size.

The existing source-linked height-store proofs and [collision lifetime note](ink-bounce-collision-lifetime.md) explain the later boundary: the mandatory movement-to-collision copy erases a fresh collision split in the same completed Mario update, with the full-return theorem's explicit zero-particle/call conditions. An inherited split is a different producer question. There is still no controller-only no-A clone/Ink witness here, no full classification of all clone histories, and no universal maximum over every interaction family. This note identifies concrete replacement sources and removes two unsupported shortcuts: distance re-entry is not enemy reallocation, and a reused slot does not preserve the donor's size.
