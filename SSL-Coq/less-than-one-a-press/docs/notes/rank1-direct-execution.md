# Ink: the real final check and completed position copy

The 4 October 2026 tranche proves three missing code connections in both
generated versions. A completed object update reaches the final platform
query; the complete normal position copy finishes with movement and collision
Y equal; and a low final collision query clears both platform references when
its actual returned floor is high. These are checked local and conditional
execution results. They do not yet exclude every gameplay producer or carry
the cleared pointers through the first Area-2 apply. Rank 1 remains open, at
the unchanged subjective 1–2% estimate.

## What changed

The final query used to have a proved function-body connection without a
proved surrounding caller. `Rank1ObjectUpdateSchedule` now follows a completed
US/JP `update_objects` call through allocation, its actual prefix, the real
`update_mario_platform` call, its tail and local free. The prefix includes
collision detection, Mario's action processing and object unloading. Their
effects are retained. There is no early return that skips the final query in
a completed object-update call. A null Mario pointer can still make the
called platform function return before its floor lookup; the full query
result separately uses a readable nonnull Mario Object. This does not prove
that every possible gameplay update calls or completes this function.

`InkRawCopyCompletion` finishes the earlier Y-store proof. Its remaining
stores write Z, angles and angular velocity. They preserve the copied Y,
MarioState Y and display Y. At return, collision Y equals movement Y, and
display Y has its original value. The claim uses the ordinary single-Mario
receiver in a valid Object-pool slot, the actual globals and readable State
height. It does not require the positions to agree on entry. It covers Y;
it is not a new all-coordinate or whole-history synchronization theorem.

`Rank1CaptureGeometry` connects the binary32 four-unit guard to height bounds,
including overflow and non-finite subtraction results. Every finite raw Y at
or below 818 fails capture of every finite returned floor at or above 1281.
The checked timer-131 height, 1938.8648681640625, satisfies that bound. This
does not infer a live floor's identity merely from a matching numerical height.

`Rank1PlatformQueryCompletion` follows the entire actual query call, including
allocation, the real `find_floor` execution and local deallocation. Under
ordinary Object-pool storage and readable coordinates, if final raw Y is
finite and at most 818, and the real returned height is finite and at least
1281, both `gMarioPlatform` and Mario's Object `platform` are null at return.
The floor lists and owner are not prescribed, and no harmless-call premise
is added for `find_floor`: its relevant effects are derived from the real
completed call. The far branch needs no owner read, even for a stale owner.
The actual returned-height bound remains a condition, not a live-list proof.

`Rank1DirectExecution` joins these results into the selected main Rank-1
boundary. It also proves that an effective later platform apply requires
changing a cleared global platform pointer. That is a necessary replacement;
it does not assume or prove that no later writer can make that change.

## Why this does not refute the working supplied controls

At X=-2200, Z=-1024, the original control has movement/collision Y=768 and
display Y=1938.8648681640625. The failed first lookup copies display to State
before action processing. The later ordinary copy can then raise collision
Y. The new clearing result concerns the final raw sample, so an earlier
collision Y of 768 is not enough to apply it.

The high-movement controls start with movement Y=1861 and collision Y=768;
display may be high or may also be 768. The first lookup can already find
the top. The disappeared action aligns movement to its selected floor;
the normal copy then raises collision. Contact has already happened by that
copy. Equal movement and collision at the copy's return can therefore help
final capture rather than forbid it. The incoming 1,093-unit split still
needs a gameplay explanation. No universal 1,170.864868-unit threshold is
introduced.

## Precise remaining connections

The existing stock motion certificate covers all 151 modeled spinning
timers, and the source distinguishes idle, spinning and exploding behavior.
It is not a live execution proof of the height bound in every phase. The
remaining bridge must follow the reached owner, home and timer, velocity
initialization, sine lookup, transform and collision loading into the real
list entry. The current guard theorem can consume any such returned-height
bound once that bridge is proved. It does not reduce all phases to timer 131.

After the final query, the generated scheduler calls a clock stub, writes its
local array, calls `try_print_debug_mario_object_info`, and updates time-stop
and object-count globals. The clock routine really returns zero without a
memory write. The debug routine depends on its reached page and printing
flags: stock page zero and disabled printing avoid its other paths, but that
stock-read connection has not been proved across the required interval.
Reached printing paths can contain external calls in this C model. The
subsequent level transition, Area-2 loading and terrain callbacks also need
their actual pointer effects; a call name is not a memory frame. These are
specific unfinished intervals, not a newly assumed no-reinstall contract.

Before that query, the decisive survivor is a high enough State or display
while the earlier collision record remains inside the warp. The existing
platform, acceptance and copy results narrow its writer and timing, but do
not classify every legitimate action/support history. Resolving it requires
the actual first producer, or a proved exclusion of a stated complete class.
This tranche starts no further Scattershot search and claims no clean route
or full Ink impossibility.
