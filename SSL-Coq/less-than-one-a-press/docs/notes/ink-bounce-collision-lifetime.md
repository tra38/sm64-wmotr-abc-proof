# How long does a bounce leave collision behind?

For this transfer test, the user grants a chosen cloned enemy position, potentially using a HOLP set in another course. We defer constructing that placement. We do not grant arbitrary dimensions, a usable intangible actor, a controller-reached Mario pose or an inherited useful split. Position is X/Y/Z; dimensions are hitbox height, radius, downward offset and the scale that updates them.

## The two copies and the next warp check

The ordinary interaction pass checks the upper warp before the bounce handlers. Accepting that warp ends the pass. A bounce cannot be added afterward in the same pass, and the ordinary action movement cannot supply a second warp check within that interval. This is why an earlier bounce, rather than a simultaneous warp/bounce, was the candidate.

The generated US/JP air step copies movement into display after its quarters, terrain, gravity and wind. The existing completed-air-return proof frames its angle setter and local cleanup: display equals movement at that return, while collision keeps its value at the copy cut. This is an air-call checkpoint, not the end of the outer Mario callback.

The new [InkMarioRawCopyCall.v](../../proofs/InkMarioRawCopyCall.v) follows the actual generated `bhv_mario_update` caller. Every completed callback reaches the real State-to-Object copy after `execute_mario_action`. Even a null-floor early return from that action helper does not skip the caller's copy. Under the ordinary pool-slot, symbol/receiver and readable-cell conditions at that cut, the completed copy makes collision Y equal movement Y and preserves display Y. We retain the actual action execution; we do not declare its calls harmless. The subsequent particle loop remains an explicit residual.

The new [InkMarioZeroParticleTail.v](../../proofs/InkMarioZeroParticleTail.v) proves that residual for an actual zero particle mask. Its checker recognizes the real loop and rejects writes/calls except the unreachable particle-spawn branch. A completed defined traversal has no memory effect, independently of the particle-table contents or number of traversed entries. With the ordinary reached-copy conditions, movement and collision therefore remain equal through callback return, and display retains its copy-cut value. This theorem does not assert that display equals movement at the outer return.

These results give a fresh bounce no later complete Mario update, and no intervening warp-interaction check, before its collision split is erased by the ordinary copy. The zero-particle case now extends that equality through callback return. This is a conditional same-callback obstruction, not an all-history closure. Other objects after Mario, the next terrain/platform phase, a different action tail, changed contact reads or an already inherited split still require their own writer/frame arguments. Nonzero particle effects are not covered by the zero-mask return theorem.

## How much separation are we talking about?

The existing actual falling-contact/snap proof bounds a fresh upward change by 251 units, including a conservative rounding allowance, when live height is at most 250, finite values satisfy its ranges and the incoming records are synchronized. This is a bound at the first snap. It is not an absolute gap bound, a flight peak or a bound on every live enemy size. In unobstructed arithmetic, movement can then add one update's upward velocity before the collision copy: approximately hitbox height plus 30 or 80, rather than the snap height alone. Floor alignment, wind and other writers are separate; no universal pre-copy maximum is claimed.

With Mario initially at synchronized Y=768, an enemy origin at the next binary32 value below 768 maximizes the fresh upward snap toward its live height. Full Pokey part, regular Goomba, ordinary Fly Guy and Klepto heights give gaps approaching 60, 75, 90 and 250. Fire Fly Guy's ordinary positive growth has a source-arithmetic candidate around 102.6. None of those named positive sizes supplies Variant's 1093-unit fresh split. The original X=-2200,Z=-1024 position is already in the warp's contact region, so that arithmetic recipe does not construct a same-pass bounce there. Preparing outside contact is a separate pose and terrain question; see the [inventory and position recipes](ssl1-clone-enemy-inventory.md).

Repeated fire-facing interruption can shrink a Fly Guy through zero and grow its negative magnitude, as the supplied video transcript describes. The positive-growth figure does not bound that magnitude. The hitbox uses signed scale, not its absolute value: radius is 70 times the newly changed X scale, while height is 60 times Y scale copied from the previous X scale at the start of the callback. Those phases must not be silently treated as uniform. A frozen clone retains existing signed dimensions; chosen HOLP position does not turn them positive. The contact's strict signed radius-sum test cannot enter its registration branch when that sum is nonpositive and distance is finite and nonnegative. The source's missing return on a failed horizontal test is not a defined route to manufacture registration.

The existing `ibp_fresh_contact_bounce_cannot_snap_below_collision` proof has no live-height ceiling: if the real passed contact top still matches the later bounce sum, the snap cannot go below the compared collision bottom. In particular, from synchronized movement/collision this blocks using a negative-height snap to manufacture a downward gap. With zero downward offset and matched reads, contact requires collision Y at most actor Y plus height, whereas the falling guard requires movement Y above actor Y; a nonpositive height cannot satisfy both from synchronized records. This is a scoped obstruction, not a proof of all size histories or contact-to-handler read continuity. The source inequalities and mixed scale phases are recorded separately in the inventory; invalid conversions and crash behavior are outside this model.

A bounce can still transform an inherited split. For example, supplied movement/display Y=1938.864868 with collision Y=768, and a regular Goomba at Y=693 with height 75, numerically snaps movement to 768 while retaining the old display at that first store. It uses the already supplied movement/collision gap; it does not create that gap from synchronized records. Warp ordering, air/display and collision copies still have to be followed. No such controller-reached setup or useful continuation is supplied here.

## Small runtime check

Wafel 0.8.5 runs the JP DLL with the previously checked controller capture. The first 499 updates reproduce its scene, and the exact scene observer matches capture poll 500 before any patch. Eight initialized stock actors are selected: three Goombas, three Fly Guys, Klepto and a Pokey body part. Each gets two supplied synchronized Mario contact poses, one and 32 units above its actual origin, with freefall, downward speed four and depth zero. Enemy position, dimensions, tangibility and RNG are untouched. Each trial restores the full scene once, applies that initial Mario patch once and runs 24 uninterrupted neutral, A-released updates.

Across **16 supplied poses**, the action-entry observer detects **12 bounces**: six Goomba and six Fly Guy contacts. All **384 end-update checkpoints** have exactly matching binary32 movement, collision and display Y. The highest detected end-update collision gap is **zero**. All first-update observed MarioState particle-flag fields are zero. This end-update read does not independently observe the caller-local mask at the copy cut. Klepto is intangible in this selected scene; the Pokey trials take a damage response. Those four non-bounces do not exclude their families. A repeated run reproduces all recorded actor, patch, input, action and position fields exactly, excluding timing.

The action-entry observer exposes movement and velocity, not raw collision/display at the snap, so this is not a measured subframe gap peak. The Goomba first end height is 156 after a 126 snap and speed 30. Fly Guy first end height is actor Y+170 after its 90-height snap and speed 80. Later ordinary copies prevent flight altitude from accumulating as an end-update collision gap in these trials. These are conditional supplied Mario poses in a controller-reached surrounding scene, not reached bounce poses, a constructed clone or a no-A route.

The primary diagnostic takes 0.794 seconds in process; the repeat takes 1.063 seconds. This is a small transfer check, not a search-throughput estimate. The committed [receipt](../../instrumentation/bounce-collision-lifetime/expected-stock-receipt.json) records the runtime, capture/DLL/report hashes, counts and first checkpoints. Full observations remain under `build/bounce-collision/20261004-stock-complete-patches/report.json` and `20261004-stock-complete-repeat/report.json`; the [README](../../instrumentation/bounce-collision-lifetime/README.md) gives reproduction commands and observation limits. Five receipt tests pass.

## Verdict

Chosen enemy placement does not bypass the falling guard, create arbitrary hitbox size or delay the mandatory collision copy. The new checked connection rules out carrying a fresh collision split through the completed ordinary copy, and through the zero-particle callback return under its reached storage conditions. It does not close the full bounce/knockback family or the complete Ink route. A later writer, inherited gap, changed contact reads and legal dimension/contact history remain distinct questions. The atlas's subjective estimates are unchanged.
