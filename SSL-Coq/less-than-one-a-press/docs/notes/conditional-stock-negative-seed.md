# A conditional stock negative-depth argument

The new theorem joins the landing arithmetic to the first-long-jump argument and a physical A-press witness. If a finite depth history starts finite and nonnegative, and satisfies the explicit classification below, a negative endpoint requires an A press somewhere in its supplied input history. Consequently a history with no A presses has no such negative endpoint.

## A fixed stopping point for this producer

The conditional implication is already proved. The work on sound, animation and earlier records has been trying to establish its conditions for gameplay, rather than adding new goals to the negative-depth question. We can use the conditional result without first removing all those conditions. On 26 September, the user requested this explicit scope review; accepting a new gameplay assumption package remains a decision to make, not a decision this review makes for them.

For **late common landing alone**, the conclusion is: starting with nonnegative depth, this write cannot create the first useful negative seed without A, provided the following conditions hold. This uses the existing `isc_landing_nonnegative_or_a` lemma; it is not a new proof.

| Condition | What we are accepting | How substantial is it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal start and tracked state | Follow the real Mario depth record from the agreed initialized, finite, nonnegative state, without starting inside the long-jump/long-jump-landing cycle. The reached write is the generated US/JP write with its actual field reads and a finite result. | The starting boundary is already agreed. Connecting the live record and arithmetic to that boundary still matters; an unexplained saved state is not granted. |
| Stock timer at the write | After the preceding calls, ordinary landing writes have timer 1–3; long-jump landing writes have timer 1–5. These are the bounds associated with duration four or six. | A specific gameplay invariant. A stock duration at initialization or a correct cancellation check alone does not establish the bound at the later write. |
| Stock first-entry history | Every long-jump landing used by the argument has an action history beginning outside the cycle and following the listed transitions. There is no additional transition, callback, state substitution or other effective action write that creates the first entry. | The largest gameplay assumption. The source checks support it, but complete coverage of actual histories is not proved. |
| Physical input connection | An A-edge event in that action history requires a real A press in the supplied controller history. | Reasonable for the intended controller model, but the complete event-to-input connection remains a condition. The current theorem needs existence of a press, not an exact-frame replay. |

For **every useful negative-depth producer**, add one more condition: every completed change to the followed depth value fits the checked writer classification, with no omitted effective write or unexplained record change. Its safe cases are reset, minimum clamp, stock quicksand increase, positive cap, early landing increase, the completed quicksand-jump clamp, death increase and preservation. Their finite arithmetic and stated no-overflow premises remain explicit. This extra coverage condition is not needed to exclude the common-landing write considered on its own; it is needed to conclude that some other writer cannot create the seed first.

Under these conditions, a first negative common-landing write must be long-jump landing at timer 4 or 5. Its action history then requires a physical A press. That is enough for the no-A exclusion; no seed-size calculation or later Ink continuation is required. The early quicksand-jump subtraction is separately excluded at its completed clamp checkpoint. Common landing has no equivalent immediate clamp.

The theorem's `no_forged_action_installs` condition is broader than “no memory corruption”: it also excludes unclassified ordinary action writers and unexpected callback or external effects that could supply a first entry. Calling the execution defined and in bounds does not automatically prove this condition. Likewise, “all calls are harmless” would be unnecessarily broad. Sound binding and the remaining call proofs are ways to justify the named timer, record and action conditions; they are not separate requirements to prove every audio or animation behavior.

**Recommended label:** “01 — Already proved, under the listed stock writer, timer and action/input conditions.” This is a useful stopping point if that restricted scope is accepted. The first-entry and writer-coverage conditions must stay visible because they exclude classes of possible gameplay exceptions; they are not an independently established background fact like ordinary arithmetic. The unconditional no-A seed claim and the Ink route remain open. This review changes the explanation, not the Coq result or the atlas estimate.

The assumption is substantial: every completed depth change must be one of the existing checked safe writer outcomes or an execution of the actual generated US/JP landing store. That execution must satisfy the stock duration gate. A long-jump landing must have a legitimate action prefix from a non-long-jump start, without forged installs, and every A edge in that prefix must have a physical controller-input witness. The safe outcomes retain their finite arithmetic and no-overflow conditions. These are explicit constructor premises, not a claim that the global inventory or its execution refinement has been discharged.

The proof follows the connected depth values through the whole finite classified sequence. At the first negative landing, the existing generated-code theorem forces long-jump landing at timer 4 or 5. The existing first-target theorem then forces an A edge, which the input refinement turns into a physical A press. The argument allows any finite number of earlier safe writes; it is not an extrapolation from sampled trials. Temporary negatives inside a writer that immediately clamps them are not exposed as endpoints here.

This settles the requested conditional claim. The necessary-condition chain is sufficient for insufficiency: useful negative depth implies late landing, and late landing implies an earlier physical A press. Once those links apply to the allowed executions, there is no need to establish the size of any A-using seed or test its later Ink continuation. Applying it to every stock gameplay history still requires proving the classification, action-prefix and input refinements. It does not settle the no-A seed search throughout the game or the Ink route. The separate course-entry reset proof remains unchanged.

Proof: [InkStockSeedConditional.v](../../proofs/InkStockSeedConditional.v), especially `isc_useful_negative_seed_requires_physical_a` and `isc_no_a_excludes_classified_negative_seed`.

## The subtraction that does not leave a seed

The 25 September writer review found that the catalog already exists: the generated US and JP source checks name the same eight direct writers, and a fresh search of the pinned C source finds no extra course-specific writer. An inventory of names alone is not the missing proof. We must connect each reached write, including its actual receiver and surrounding calls, to the outcomes used by the conditional theorem.

[InkJumpClamp.v](../../proofs/InkJumpClamp.v) supplies one of those connections. In the real `quicksand_jump_land_action`, an incoming timer from 0 through 5 takes the early branch. The code increments it first, so the subtraction uses 1 through 6. A raw result below 1 is replaced with the game's binary32 value for 1.1 before the sound and animation calls. The proof follows the actual field reads and stores, not a replacement arithmetic routine.

The completed-call theorem, `ijc_completed_early_call_clamps_before_helpers`, retains that call's entry, timer increment, subtraction, clamp, helper suffix, movement suffix and return. At the checkpoint before the helpers, the stored depth is finite and nonnegative, assuming the raw subtraction is finite and the MarioState offsets do not wrap. `ijc_pair_extends_classified_history` connects the actual store pair to the existing safe-writer constructor. No premise says that later sound, animation or movement leaves depth alone, and no conclusion says that the final returned depth is safe regardless of those later calls.

This closes the temporary-subtraction candidate at that checkpoint. It does not discharge every writer's live receiver, arithmetic bounds, action/timer history or controller-edge refinement. The late common-landing calculation remains the substantive producer to trace; no new no-A seed or complete impossibility result is claimed.

The [late-landing investigation](late-common-landing-investigation.md) preserves the separate descriptor throughout continuing cancellation and now carries the returned bound directly into six real landing-body calls. It connects completed animation to its actual loader: a zero return preserves the timer, while a changed timer must be present in the reached transfer's returned memory. The central setter cannot convert a different request into a long jump, and other direct safe-literal action assignments cannot construct either long-jump action. The descriptor-use inventory also corrects a possible shortcut: all nine records are exported, despite their restricted ordinary reads, so the private-table theorem does not settle their lifetime. The stock sound-request implementation now has a proved memory frame, and initialization's own action store is proved to choose only standing or water idle. The sound body still needs binding into the selected gameplay runtime. Earlier descriptor protection, other timer effects and the first-request/input history remain open. The seed-to-A theorem therefore retains its stated conditions. The one-A controls and matching no-A misses remain finite observations; the linked investigation records the current audit.

An immediate clamp before any relevant gameplay read is sufficient to exclude that writer as a useful seed producer. This is why the early quicksand-jump result counts as a disproof within its conditions. Common landing ends with its depth write and return, without the same clamp; the later sinking code can read depth. Its no-A producer cannot be rejected by borrowing the other function's clamp. This distinction does not grant a reachable late timer or a negative seed.
