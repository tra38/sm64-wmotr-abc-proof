# SSL Area 1: placements for tornadoes, bombs, fire and boxes

This is a source review with small binary32 arithmetic diagnostics. It is not a new runtime witness, a Coq certificate for the new calculations, or a full impossibility proof. The user's chosen cloned-enemy X/Y/Z grant supplies position, not arbitrary dimensions, velocity, home coordinates, tangibility, a resumed native callback, or an earlier position split. The clone/HOLP route remains separate.

The reference C is pinned to `9921382a68bb0c865e5e45eb594d9c64db59b1af`. The current decomp checkout is at `36fbf8d693a9fc2bdec0c77402f8e96d07d2f461`; comparison against the reference found no substantive changes in the C functions reviewed here. The actual generated US/JP bodies were also inspected. They remain the program used for formal claims.

Let **M**, **C** and **D** be movement, raw collision and display Y. The fresh-placement comparison starts with all three equal to **768**. For a different starting height, substitute that height in the formulas. Actor height, Mario's collision height, incoming velocity, floor history and contact timing are independent inputs; choosing actor position does not choose them.

The normal Mario hitbox is **160** units high. Actions with `ACT_FLAG_SHORT_HITBOX`, including crouching/crawling, use **100**. The comparison concerns a signed upward split; it is not a universal absolute-gap bound.

None of the direct writes above is an already established fresh producer of those splits. A mechanism can nevertheless raise Mario's altitude over several updates while the copies keep the three records together.

## A tornado catches relative height, not its top

`interact_tornado` records `oMarioTornadoPosY = M - actorY`. It does not set Mario's movement Y to the actor's origin or hitbox top. The selected automatic action also has no launch constructor that silently replaces this subtraction. Incoming vertical speed survives capture, while forward speed is set to0.

In `act_tornado_twirling`, speed below60 is increased by1; speed already60 or higher is retained. Thus60 is not a universal incoming-speed cap. The stored relative height is increased by that new speed and clamped to0 if it becomes negative. If the result exceeds the actor's live hitbox height, the action ejects before assigning the proposed position. Otherwise the proposed Y is the actor's current origin plus the updated relative height.

For unchanged actor origin E and matched reads, the single-precision calculation is:

```
relative_on_capture = f32(M - E)
v_next = v < 60 ? f32(v + 1) : v
relative_next = max(0, f32(relative_on_capture + v_next))
proposed_Y = f32(E + relative_next)
```

This explains why putting the actor thousands of units above Mario is ineffective for a fresh contact. With zero actor down offset, the passed vertical-overlap test requires E no higher than Mario's tested raw top, `f32(C + MarioHitboxHeight)`. From synchronized records, that top is928 for normal Mario at768. Choosing E=928 leaves relative height−160. With speed0, the next relative height clamps to0 and proposed Y becomes928. The short-hitbox version uses E=868 and gives100 instead.

This is a different placement from the earlier enemy-bounce recipe, which puts an enemy just *below* Mario to pass hit-from-above. Tornado capture has no such hit-from-above requirement. Horizontal overlap is still strict, tangibility is still required and the overlap records/read identities must still match. The native Tweester template is radius1500/height4000/down offset0; ordinary full-growth scale gives live height about800–1600 and radius about600–780. A frozen initialized clone retains its existing dimensions, rather than choosing the template height4000 as an arbitrary live height.

In real-number arithmetic with unchanged E, the direct upward displacement is no more than the greater of the fresh Mario span and `v_next`; it is **not** span plus speed. If incoming speed is at most60, the increment branch can approach61. The160-span clamp therefore dominates that example. This explanatory inequality is not yet a rounding-certified Coq bound over every finite live value. The exact integer examples above use binary32 arithmetic and have no rounding ambiguity.

There are two distinct escapes from this calculation that should stay visible. The actor's origin can change between capture and later action reads, contributing that change to Mario's motion. Also, when the proposed-position floor query misses, the action writes the larger of proposed Y and the remembered `m->floorHeight`. That remembered height can dominate the tornado formula. An unexplained high cached floor would inherit the floor-alignment producer; it is not justified by the actor-position grant. Wall correction and a successful query can affect which position/floor is used. No new global maximum follows from the160 example.

For scale, an illustrative fixed-origin H=1600 profile, E=928, incoming speed0, no wall/cache interference and accepted non-ejecting position branches reaches M=1962 at update45. Its largest single-update M increase is160 on the initial clamp; later upward updates add at most60 in that profile. With H=800 it reaches1707 at update39 and takes the early ejection branch on update40. These are arithmetic traces, not emulator detections or controller routes. They show that usable *altitude* and a usable *record disagreement* are different questions.

The existing Coq theorem `twg_non_ejecting_continuation_has_no_gap_checkpoint` in [TweesterGap.v](../../proofs/TweesterGap.v) follows both actual floor outcomes to the normal display copy. At its completed copy, M=D. It does not require a successful floor lookup. Early ejection is separately outside that theorem; source inspection shows it returns before the position assignment, then changes to an ordinary air action. The mandatory callback raw-copy result applies to the completed callback under its reached storage conditions; its zero-particle return extension carries M=C through return when that actual mask is0. None of these facts supplies the earlier clone route or rules out every intervening writer before the next update.

At the supplied low warp-contact X/Z, an ordinary accepted warp handler runs before the tornado handler and stops the interaction pass. Also, the checked actual Y=768 pose has a failed pre-action floor lookup; it cannot be treated as a supported place to execute forty-five tornado updates. The sizing recipe therefore needs a real supported/correctly dispatched context outside already accepted warp contact before transport is considered.

## Bombs and explosions change the action, not the height

An initialized stock Bob-omb is grabbable/kickable, with nominal radius65/height113. Its swelling native phase changes scale before it spawns the separate explosion. That does not convert it into a top-bounce interaction. The explosion script installs `INTERACT_DAMAGE`, damage2, radius150, height150 and down offset150. Its growing graphical scale does not replace those directly installed hitbox fields. Moving that damaging volume can arrange overlap; it does not install an enemy-top snap in Mario.

`take_damage_and_knock_back` selects the action through `determine_knockback_action`. In dry ground/air cases, that selector changes facing and ensures a horizontal speed of at least16. The airborne knockback constructors do not install a new upward vertical speed. The water selector has a conditional speed20 adjustment, but neither dry explosion placement nor SSL pyramid placement grants a water action. Big-knockback subtype gives forward speed40, not a vertical launch.

Ground knockback then calls `perform_ground_step`; airborne knockback calls `perform_air_step`. A prior positive vertical speed can produce upward travel during the latter, but that speed needs its own source. A changed floor can produce a ground snap, but that floor needs its own source. There is no unique height-optimizing bomb position for a direct Y write: the direct Y contribution is0 for every placement that reaches these reviewed dry handler branches. This is a reviewed source fact, not a new theorem framing every sound/drop/damage helper's memory effects.

## Fire provides a small no-A launch

`interact_flame` can select burning jump without an A press when Mario is grounded or is still rising in an airborne action. For an airborne action with nonpositive vertical speed it instead selects burning fall, which does not install the jump speed. Invulnerability, metal/vanish cap and the delay-invincibility subtype can block it; swimming or more than50 units of water above Mario can extinguish it. A chosen flame location does not remove these guards.

The burning-jump constructor writes31.5 to vertical speed and8 to forward speed, but does not move Mario immediately. A clear dry burning jump follows the gravity4 branch. Eight positive updates have speeds31.5,27.5,23.5,19.5,15.5,11.5,7.5,3.5 and raise768 to908: **140 altitude**, not a140 retained display gap. Each actual air step copies movement to display; the callback then has its mandatory raw copy. The first clear step's transient M−C is31.5. Floor/ceiling/wall outcomes can interrupt this profile and vertical wind or another later action needs its own analysis, so140 is not an unrestricted flight maximum.

The native piranha-fire template is radius10/height20/down offset10 before scale. SSL's fire-spitting actors can spawn these flames. Flame size determines contact availability, not the31.5 launch magnitude. Burning actions request `PARTICLE_FIRE`, so the zero-particle callback-return theorem must not be applied to them. The copy checkpoint remains mandatory; framing the actual nonzero-particle tail is a separate obligation. Once Mario lands in burning ground, a fresh burning jump from that action requires `INPUT_A_PRESSED`. Simply supplying another flame during the invulnerable burning continuation does not grant a repeat launch.

## The fire-spitter body and its flame are different actors

The two stock bodies start at `(-386,512,-2228)` and `(-5125,135,-3138)`. Their script installs graphical flags, billboard mode and scale0.4, then calls the native fire-spitter update. ## A held jumping box really can launch Mario without A

The two stock jumping boxes start at `(1120,0,6480)` and `(-5200,0,1700)`. Their free native update fixes scale0.5, giving radius75, height125 and down offset10 from the grabbable template. Their script marks them holdable. Ordinary grabbing is a legitimate stock mechanic, unlike granting an arbitrary already-held box or substituting its behavior pointer. Once Mario actually holds this species and enters `act_hold_walking`, the first check selects `ACT_CRAZY_BOX_BOUNCE` with argument0, before its A-press check.

At action timer0, that air action installs vertical speed45/60/100 for arguments0/1/2 and minimum forward speed32/36/48. These stores have no A guard. A landing during stage0 or1 selects the same action with the next argument, resetting the next launch; the stage2 landing releases/breaks the box and selects stomach slide. Consequently the100 launch is not available simply because an arbitrary box was placed underneath Mario: it needs legitimate pickup and the two preceding landings. Chosen actor X/Y/Z does not modify the three speeds.

The action's flags omit `ACT_FLAG_CONTROL_JUMP_HEIGHT`, so merely releasing A does not invoke the strengthened jump-ascent gravity rule for this action. In the ordinary clear-air gravity4 profile, four quarter-step additions of `v/4` followed by `v -= 4` give these exact binary32 arithmetic results:

| Launch speed | Positive updates | Altitude rise | Peak from Y768 | First clear step's transient M−C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 45 | 12 | 276 | 1044 | 45 |
| 60 | 15 | 480 | 1248 | 60 |
| 100 | 25 | **1300** | **2068** | 100 |

These calculations were evaluated with a binary32 rounding operation after each quarter-step addition and gravity subtraction. They are arithmetic diagnostics, not a new Coq envelope or game replay. Walls, ceilings, floors and changes of action can interrupt the profile; wind or another inherited state needs its own conditions. There is no all-history theoretical maximum here. The important distinction is that the third-stage altitude rise is large enough to exceed1093 and1170.864868, while each completed `perform_air_step` has its real movement-to-display copy and the Mario callback has its later movement-to-collision copy. Those copies stop the ordinary flight rise accumulating into a disagreement with an old low record. A separate writer or skipped action/copy would still need explaining.

## The other stock boxes do not inherit that launch

The small grabbable box at `(5900,50,3440)` initializes scale0.4 and live radius60/height100/down offset8. Its thrown-object speeds are writes to the box. Its behavior pointer is `bhvBreakableBoxSmall`, so holding it does not satisfy the jumping-box identity check above. Its normal grabbable/push handler has no direct Mario Y snap or crazy-box launch. ## Tox Boxes are floors, not giant bounce hitboxes

The native Tox Box script loads a solid collision mesh and adds256 to its initial Y before recording home. Its mesh coordinates run from−255 to256 on each axis, with a hollow underside. The native roll writes the actor's Y from home, sine-based motion and a subsequent transform move. The face pitch/roll changes are not writes to the angular-velocity fields used by Mario's displacement helper. Position grant alone does not choose a new homeY; allowing the native callback to resume may replace the chosen Y with its ordinary home-based path.

For actual zero pitch/yaw/roll angular velocity, the platform helper adds X/Z velocity and retains its local Y. An arbitrarily moved platform origin therefore does not create an arbitrarily large Mario height split through that helper. Existing [InkPlatformProducerHeight.v](../../proofs/InkPlatformProducerHeight.v) and [InkPlatformMovement.v](../../proofs/InkPlatformMovement.v) provide the scoped setter/copy and full-phase preservation results. Live zero-field provenance and rider ownership still matter.

The final platform query boards an owned floor only when raw Mario is less than4 units from its queried height. Placing a box top1100 units above raw Y768 cannot satisfy that boarding check. Ground movement can follow a newly selected floor instead, but that is the floor-alignment family with its live-query, remembered-height and mandatory-copy obligations. Mesh extent alone is not a bound on every later selected floor or stale cached floor.

Fake pickup of a non-holdable box switches its current behavior command to a carry script that executes `BREAK`. That preserves existing fields but does not automatically execute `bhv_tox_box_loop` and reload the mesh at the chosen new position. Under the ordinary non-time-stopped update, dynamic surfaces are cleared before terrain callbacks reload them. During active time stop the clear is skipped, so prior loaded surfaces can persist; they are prior transformed geometry, not a grant of newly repositioned collision. This is why a frozen-clone support claim needs a real floor-list/callback history. It is not already covered by a visual-position grant or by saying that a Tox Box is solid in the stock game.

## Theoretical sizing and detected gaps are separate

| Quantity | This review establishes | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fresh Tweester clamp | Conditional exact160/100 placement calculation with zero incoming speed and matched unchanged origin; later normal display-copy checkpoint is already proved | Global maximum with changing origin, inherited velocity or remembered floor; no new emulator capture witness |
| Burning jump | Exact clear-air arithmetic rise140 and first-step displacement31.5 | Full-history flight envelope, nonzero-particle callback frame or an installed Ink gap |
| Jumping box | Real stock no-A launch constructors45/60/100; exact clear-air rises276/480/1300 | Controller-only access to the required timed three-stage flight at the top, or a retained1300 record split |

| Tox / large-box support | A live floor/collision question; actual zero-angular-velocity platform displacement has no Y translation | A general stale-floor or rotating-support bound from arbitrary actor placement |

The companion [placement runtime review](ssl1-placement-runtime-review.md) reports **24** chosen-position conditional poses and **72** checkpoints. Their largest end-of-update movement/collision gap was **0**.
